# VISUAL CANON
### Photography, painting, fashion, architecture. The image-library behind the frames.
### Each entry names the specific body of work — never just the artist.

The through-line that resolves the central tension (queer-longing male body vs. classical-power male body): **the idealized European boy as object of unresolved desire.** Hedi Slimane's Dior Homme is the contemporary node where both readings collapse into one silhouette. You don't resolve the tension — it IS the content.

---

## THE TWO CENTRAL PILLARS

**Vilhelm Hammershøi — the *Strandgade 30* interiors (1898–1909).**
60+ canvases of his own sparse Copenhagen apartment: grey-on-grey, a single black-dressed figure (his wife Ida) seen from behind. *Moonlight, Strandgade 30* (Met); *Interior. The Music Room, Strandgade 30*. The painter of "silence and light." This is the entire mood in one body of work — Scandinavian emptiness, restraint, architecture as character, the back-turned figure of sovereign indifference. *Source: Met, Art Institute Chicago, SMK Copenhagen; Artvee for high-res public-domain files.*

**Hedi Slimane — Dior Homme (2000–2007).**
The pale, thin, unsmiling all-black European boy with hollow cheeks; jeans painted-on tight, leather jacket worn like armor. Slimane shifted the whole decade from 90s muscle toward androgynous alienation-as-luxury. **This silhouette is where queer-longing and classical-idealized-form become the same body** — the contemporary engine of the aesthetic. (His later Saint Laurent and Celine continue it.) *Source: Dior runway archive; "Hedi Slimane: Anthology of a Decade"; the Dior Homme 2000–2007 Pinterest/are.na boards.*

## I. Photographers — the male body, youth, longing

- **Collier Schorr — *Neighbors / Nachbarn* (1991–2006), *Forests and Fields*.** German village youths posed in real/fictional military uniforms. **The unresolved tension made literal: the beautiful boy as both desire-object and uniformed power-figure, deliberately undecided.** *303 Gallery; MACK books.*
- **Peter Hujar — *Portraits in Life and Death* (1976).** Downtown NY portraits facing the Palermo catacomb corpses. Stillness, mortality, the male nude with cold tenderness — dangerous calm at its source. *Peter Hujar Archive / Fraenkel.*
- **Mark Morrisroe — Boston School Polaroids & sandwich-negative prints (1980s).** Sickly, romantic, decaying-edge gay portraiture; beauty that already knows it's dying. The melancholic counterweight to the clean surface. *Estate at Ringier / Fotomuseum Winterthur.*
- **Luigi Ghirri — *Kodachrome* (1970–78).** Italian color, muted palette, tight geometry, "deleted space." Empty piazzas and golden-hour ennui — negative-space and architecture-as-character in photographic form. *Matthew Marks; MACK reissue.*
- **Wolfgang Tillmans — *Lutz & Alex* and early Cologne/London portraits (early–mid 90s).** Tender, unposed European youth, sun-faded intimacy, no spectacle. The "expensive nothing" of bodies just existing in light. *Maureen Paley / David Zwirner.*
- **Bruce Weber — *Bear Pond* (1990) / early Calvin Klein.** Classical idealized male body as Arcadian fantasy — the idealization node coded as American homoeroticism. Use carefully; it's exactly the ambiguity the saves circle. *photo-book market / AmericanSuburbX.*
- **Larry Clark — *Tulsa* (1971) / *Teenage Lust* (1983).** Raw, dangerous adolescence — the un-aestheticized edge that keeps the work from going purely decorative. *MoCP; Grove Press.*
- **Helmut Newton — *Big Nudes* / 1970s Vogue Paris.** Less for the subjects than the temperature: glossy, sovereign, predatory stillness. The cold-menace register. *Helmut Newton Foundation, Berlin.*

## II. Painters — solitude, chiaroscuro, empty interiors

- **Caspar David Friedrich — *Wanderer above the Sea of Fog* (c.1818), the Rückenfigur paintings.** The back-turned solitary figure commanding a sublime void — the literal posture of sovereign solitude. (Note: also the most far-right-appropriated of these — relevant to the power/desire tension.) *Hamburger Kunsthalle.*
- **Caravaggio — *Amor Vincit Omnia* (1602), *Bacchus*, the *John the Baptist* variants.** Chiaroscuro male body — sacred and erotic in one brushstroke, lit out of total darkness. The Renaissance origin of "beautiful boy emerging from black." *Gemäldegalerie Berlin; Galleria Borghese.*
- **Classical / Hellenistic statuary — the *Antinous* type, the *Doryphoros*, the *Belvedere Torso*.** The idealized male nude as both Greek desire (Antinous = Hadrian's deified lover) AND the form later appropriated for fascist "discipline." **Antinous specifically holds both readings — the most precise single object for this taste.** *Museo Nazionale Romano, Vatican, British Museum.*
- **(Use sparingly) Edward Hopper — *Morning Sun*, *Sun in an Empty Room* (1963).** American light-on-empty-wall solitude. Powerful but obvious/overused — seasoning, not centerpiece.

## III. Fashion editorial / houses

- **Helmut Lang archive — 1986–2005 (esp. early-90s minimalism).** Essentialist, not just minimal: industrial fabrics, bondage straps, paint-stained denim, bulletproof vests. The cold, narrow, slightly-fetish restraint under the clean surface. *MAK Vienna "Séance de Travail"; @helmutlangarchive.*
- **Raf Simons — *Riot Riot Riot* (S/S 2001), late-90s/early-00s.** Gawky pale teenage boys, oversized tailoring, post-punk Belgian melancholy. The intellectual, literary cousin of Slimane's silhouette. *are.na / "Raf Simons Redux."*
- **Maison Martin Margiela — early collections, white-coat anonymity, *Artisanal*.** Facelessness, deconstruction, refusal of spectacle = sovereign indifference as brand philosophy. *MoMu Antwerp.*
- **Prada / Miuccia — late-90s to early-00s ("ugly chic" / sludgy palette).** Restraint with a faint wrongness; expensive nothing. *Vogue Runway archive.*
- **032c & i-D / Dazed editorial mood (late 2000s–2010s).** The magazine grammar of this aesthetic: Berlin austerity, cold European editorial, intellectual-fashion tone. 032c's Brutalist layout is itself a reference. *032c.com; NSS Magazine.*

## IV. Architecture / space — the empty composed interior

- **Tadao Ando — *Church of the Light* (Ibaraki, 1989).** Three materials only: concrete, glass, light. A cruciform slit of light in a black concrete void. Sacred minimalism; the dangerous-calm room. *ArchDaily; Phaidon.*
- **Brutalism — béton brut interiors (Le Corbusier's *La Tourette*, the Barbican, Yugoslav modernism).** Raw concrete as mood: monastic, severe, sovereign. The masculine-discipline register of the empty room. *SOS Brutalism database; are.na brutalism channels.*
- **Scandinavian / Nordic minimalism.** Pale wood, white walls, single chair, north light — the Hammershøi room modernized. The Scandinavian bedroom of the ICP. *Kinfolk archive; danslegris.*
- **Parisian Haussmann apartment + Roman ruin (recurring set-pieces).** Herringbone floors, tall windows, golden-hour shadow; broken classical stone. Architecture-as-character. *film stills + are.na "Parisian apartment" / "ruins."*

## V. The spine (if you need a thesis)

The pull between **CMBYN (queer longing)** and **Breker/Riefenstahl (classical-power body)** is not a contradiction to resolve — it's the content. Three works sit exactly on the fault line:
- **Collier Schorr's *Neighbors*** — the desired boy literally put in the uniform.
- **Antinous statuary** — the same marble body is Hadrian's lover AND the fascist "ideal."
- **Beau Travail** (film) — the male body as erotic spectacle and military discipline, unresolved by design.
